# Rewriting each part

Read this before writing step 3. Every example uses a made-up account. Keep facts the person gave you; never invent a result, a client or a number for the proof line. If there is no proof, ask for one or leave the line out.

## Name field

Search matches the text of the name field as well as the handle, so the name field is the one place on the profile that can hold a search phrase without spending bio characters.

Pattern: `<the name people know> | <what someone would search>`

- `Ana Ruiz | Meal prep for one`
- `Kofi Mensah | Small business taxes`
- `Lume Studio | Lisbon wedding photos`

Rules:
- Plain letters. "Font" letters from generator sites look different but search reads the underlying characters, so a name in them may not match what people type.
- The search phrase, not a slogan: what someone types, not how they'd describe themselves.
- Stay near 30 characters so it reads at a glance.
- Choose it once: name changes can be limited, so don't suggest trying variations on the live account.

## Bio (150 characters)

Three jobs, in this order. Each can be one short line.

1. **Who it's for and what they get.** Audience + payoff. "Cheap dinners for people who cook for one" beats "Food lover | Home cook | Dreamer".
2. **One proof.** A result, a number, a credential, years, a known client. "Fed 4,000 readers on $50 a week" or "Ex-chef, 12 years". Only facts the person gave you.
3. **One ask, with its reason, matching the first link.** "Free 7-day plan below" when the first link is that plan. "DM 'START' for the checklist" when a DM keyword is set up.

Search words go in as plain words inside those lines, not as a hashtag list. Location goes in for anything local.

Write two or three versions that differ in angle (outcome-led, audience-led, proof-led), each counted by `check` against 150. Line breaks count as characters.

Skip: quotes, identity lists ("Dad | Runner | Coffee"), emoji used as bullets on every line, "DM for collabs" as the only ask, and "link below" with no reason to tap.

## Links

Up to 5; only the first shows on the profile. Put the destination that matches the bio's ask first and give it a title that says what they get ("Free 7-day plan", not "Website"). A link typed into the bio text isn't clickable; move it to Links.

## Highlights

Highlights keep stories on the profile after they expire, so they work as the profile's menu. Titles: one or two plain words that show in full under the circle. Order for a newcomer:

1. `Start here` / `About`: who this is, what you'll get, in 3 to 5 stories.
2. `Results` / `Reviews`: proof, screenshots of messages, before and after.
3. The main topic or offer: `Recipes`, `Tax tips`, `Book a call`.
4. Optional: `FAQ`, `Behind the scenes`.

If the person has no highlights, tell them which stories to make first (start with `Start here`), not only what to call them. Covers: one simple icon or colour set, so titles do the explaining.

## Pinned posts (up to 3)

The first row a newcomer sees. Give the three pins three different jobs:

1. **Intro:** who this is and what the account is about. Often a post that names the topic in its cover.
2. **Proof:** the post with the most shares or saves, or the best result.
3. **Way in:** the offer, the free thing, or the post that best shows the ask in the bio.

Pick from existing posts when one fits; name it by its cover text and say which job it does. When none fits a job, describe the post to make in one or two sentences (what it says, what the cover reads).

## Handle

Change it only if it loses real points: hard to spell when heard, unrelated to the name people know. Say the cost plainly: followers may be notified, large accounts may need review, links and mentions elsewhere stop pointing to it. Offer one or two options that are short, sayable, and close to the name.

## Things text can't fix

One concrete instruction each:

- **Photo:** face filling most of the circle, plain background, high contrast; a simple logo for a brand.
- **Covers:** keep the subject and any text in the centre, since the grid shows a tall crop of each cover. If the app offers "Reorder grid", the strongest posts can move up.
- **Grid topic:** if the top rows mix topics, the next posts should stay on the main one until it reads at a glance.
- **Posting:** a pace they can keep; post again before the gap passes two weeks.
- **Account type:** a creator or business account if they want to grow; show the category only if it fits.
